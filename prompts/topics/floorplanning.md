Cover at least: inputs and outputs of floorplanning; die vs. core area, aspect ratio and core utilization; IO pin and pad placement; macro placement guidelines (data flow, keeping macros at edges, channel spacing, orientation, pin access, halos); placement rows and sites; well-tap and endcap cells; placement blockages; power planning (rings, straps/stripes, standard-cell rails, PDN), IR drop and electromigration at a conceptual level; hierarchical floorplanning and partitioning basics; how floorplan decisions affect placement, timing and congestion.

Scope: include macro placement, IO planning and power grid design. Leave standard-cell placement to placement, clock tree construction to CTS, and signal routing to routing.

App context: the student sees an animated floorplan that builds up step by step: die and core outlines with placement rows, IO pins along the die edges, macros placed with halos, then the power grid (core rings, vertical and horizontal straps, and standard-cell rails), with live stats such as core utilization and aspect ratio. In the app, core utilization means total instance area (standard cells plus macros) divided by core area, and the markers on the die edges are logical IO pins (not pad cells); the skill should note that other tools and reports may define these differently.
